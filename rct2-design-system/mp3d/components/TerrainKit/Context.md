# TerrainKit

**CANONICAL IMPORT — copy exactly:** `import { buildTerrain, buildSurround } from './components/TerrainKit';`
*(never from `'./Park'`: only the `<Park>` wrappers + track-piece JSX live there. A wrong
specifier makes esbuild refuse the WHOLE bundle — the round-8 black-page failure.)*

Sculpted terrain heightfield for building whole park landscapes.

`buildTerrain(t, opts?)` returns `{ mesh, heightAt(x, z), size }`: a displaced PlaneGeometry driven by deterministic FBM value-noise (hashed sines, no Math.random) plus authored `peaks` (hills) and `basins` (lake bowls carved below `waterLevel`). `heightAt` matches the mesh exactly — use it to conform rocks, trees, paths and rides to the ground. Colouring blends an aqua-teal underwater basin (0x4fb0c2 at depth into bright sand at the waterline — submerged ground reads as tropical shallows through the water, never slate), a foam waterline, shore sand, grass and grey rock strata on steep slopes via smoothed vertex colours; a mud skirt seals the tile edges (`edgeSkirt: false` when a `buildSurround` ring continues the land).

**THE GRASS (reworked 2026-07 — it used to render as a pale untextured clay slab).** Four things carry the turf now, and they are layered deliberately:
1. **The palette is sRGB-DECODED** (`col()` / the exported `terrainPaletteRGB`). Vertex colours go into a buffer three.js treats as LINEAR, and `ColorManagement` is on by default in r152+, so the terrain was the ONE surface in a park rendering its palette uncorrected: 0x5f7a3e came out at rgb 166/184/135, a pale sage, while the trees standing on it rendered correctly. `ParkBuilder/dressing.ts`'s climate tint uses the SAME decode — the two must agree or the tint drags the ground back toward pale.
2. **PATCHINESS** — FOUR octaves of the heightfield's own value noise (`patchAt`), mixing a straw-green `TURF_PALE` against a dark `TURF_DARK`. This is the variation you read from the orbit camera, and it is keyed on world XZ so it never tiles and never aliases. **Beware the wavelengths:** `vnoise`'s slowest term has a period of ~15 in its ARGUMENT, so `x / 13.7` is a **205-u** feature, not a 14-u one — the three original octaves ran at ~200 / ~80 / ~30 u, which across a 128-u plot is barely more than a gradient, and the entire 1-20 u "clumps and patches" band was left to the tiled detail maps. The fourth octave (`x / 0.85`, ~13 u and ~3.6 u) puts it back where it belongs.
3. **HOLLOWS DAMP / CRESTS BLEACHED** — each vertex's height relative to the ground ±6 u away. Water collects in dips and drains off rises; it is the cue that makes relief legible from above.
4. **A NEUTRAL-GREY TURF DETAIL MAP** (`turfTexture`, one **1024²** canvas per process, shared, reused as the bump map at `bumpScale` 0.22, one tile per **12 world units** = 85 texels/u). Being GREY it MULTIPLIES the vertex tone, so sand stays sand and rock stays rock — the previous code deleted its colour map outright (`map = null`) to avoid exactly that staining, and so had no sub-vertex detail at all. Its mean luminance is measured at build time and cancelled in `material.color`, so adding it does not darken the park; only the VARIATION is new. Everything in it is above ~11 cycles per tile ON PURPOSE — a texture's LOW-frequency content is what makes tiling legible, and a 4-cycle mottle drew a visible 8-u grid across the whole plot in the nadir shot.

5. **A FINE BLADE MAP MULTIPLIED INTO THE SAME COLOUR** (`turfBladeCanvas` / `applyTurfBlade`, one more 512² canvas per process, shared, one tile per **2 world units**). See the audit below — item 4 alone is a MACRO map and cannot serve a close camera.

**AUDIT — "grass on terrain looks way too pixelated when zoomed in" (2026-07).**
*Measured cause.* Turf texel density is `TURF_TEX_S / TURF_TILE_U` = 512 / 12 = **42.7 texels per world unit**, and it does NOT improve on a small plot — the repeat scales with `size`, so every plot ≥ 24 u gets the same 42.7 (a 12-u standalone tile gets 85.3 only because `turfRepeat` is floored at 2). Park preview 0 (48-u plot) at a 3-u camera on a 700-px canvas puts 383 screen px on a world unit: **8.97 screen pixels per texel**, with the map's finest content (the 79-cycle grain, 6.5 texels wide) spread over 58 screen pixels. Bilinear magnification of that is precisely the reported "large soft blocks". Filtering was NOT the cause — mipmaps were on, `anisotropy` was already 8, `magFilter` was the default linear.
*What was tried and rejected.* (a) Raising `TURF_TEX_S`: 42.7 → 341 texels/u needs a 4096² canvas; measured build time for the same pixel loop is 77 ms at 512², 285 ms at 1024², **1108 ms at 2048²**, and because `turfTexture` mints one THREE.Texture per repeat, every distinct plot size in the app pays the upload again — 22 MB each at 2048². (b) Tightening `TURF_TILE_U`: an 8-u tile already drew a visible lattice (see item 4), 2 u would be far worse for a map that carries low-frequency mottle. (c) **Putting the fine map in `bumpMap`** at its own repeat (three ≥ r151 gives every slot its own uv transform): rendered at `bumpScale` 0.09 AND at 2.0 and changed almost nothing in either shot — on a `roughness: 1` diffuse lawn under a high sun plus hemisphere fill, that normal wobble barely moves N·L. The eye reads the COLOUR map, so the colour map is where the detail has to go.
*The fix.* A second, high-frequency-ONLY 512² canvas tiled every 2 u (**256 texels/u**, 1.50 screen px per texel at the same camera, coarsest feature 1/23 of a tile) multiplied into `diffuseColor` by a four-line `onBeforeCompile` on the ground material only, with its raw mean divided back out and a `TURF_BLADE_MIX` of 0.85 so the two contrasts do not compound into soot. It tiles 6× tighter than the macro map WITHOUT drawing a lattice for exactly the reason item 4 gives: legible tiling comes from low-frequency content lining up, and there is none in it. `customProgramCacheKey` is set — three's program cache does not hash `onBeforeCompile`.
*Cost.* +1 texture (512² RGBA, 1.05 MB, 1.40 MB with mips) shared by every terrain; +81 ms of one-time canvas build; one extra `texture2D` per ground fragment. Park preview 0: draw calls 588 → 576, triangles unchanged, scene texture memory 24.04 → 25.38 MB, `validatePark` `ok: true` both sides, fps within run-to-run noise. Frame mean brightness 63.7 → 63.1 (the mean-cancellation holds), stdev 14.6 → 16.6. **Nothing in Stage's shared texture library changed** — `drawTexture`'s 128² default and every `wood`/`concrete`/`metal`/`grass` call site are untouched, and Stage's own grass ground disc measures 120 texels/u with 1-px stroke content, which reads fine up close and needed no fix.

**AUDIT — "the grass looks too pixelated still and noisy" (2026-07, the pass after the one above).**
*The number that decides this is not the map's byte range, it is the RMS of the MULTIPLIER the maps apply to `diffuseColor` in LINEAR space.* Measured on the build the user rejected: the macro map's 66-255 byte range decodes (it is tagged `SRGBColorSpace`, and the decode roughly DOUBLES relative contrast) to a **0.13 .. 2.44 multiplier, RMS 0.433**; the blade map at `TURF_BLADE_MIX` 0.85 to **0.25 .. 1.85, RMS 0.275**; the two MULTIPLY, for a **combined per-texel albedo RMS of 0.53**. That is static, not turf — and the render agreed: green tweed from the nadir, camouflage at mid distance. Three separable causes, all measured:
- **Amplitude.** Both maps were ~3× too strong (above).
- **Content at texel scale, then magnified.** The macro map's LARGEST-weighted term (0.95) was a 79-cycle grain = 6.5 texels a cell at 512², i.e. per-texel noise; magnifying per-texel noise is what "pixelated" means, and at 42 texels/u it also sat at ~1 screen pixel from the MID cameras, where no mip level can help. It is gone; 53 cycles (19 texels) is the finest macro content now, and `TURF_TEX_S` is 1024 so a texel covers 6 screen px at the closest camera instead of 12.
- **An incoherent PER-VERTEX hash.** `fract(sin(x*12.9898 + z*78.233) * 43758.5453)` at ±3.75% ran once per vertex — 0.45 u apart on a 128-u plot, i.e. **5-6 screen pixels** from the orbit/nadir cameras, fully decorrelated, on a regular grid, Gouraud-interpolated. That is a WOVEN pattern by construction and it is what made the top-down shot read as fabric; a detail map cannot hide it because it lives in the vertex buffer and has no mip chain. Replaced by two octaves of the file's smooth `vnoise` at 1.1 u / 0.42 u and less than half the amplitude.
*Where the detail went instead.* Fine content is safe in the BLADE map and unsafe in the macro map, purely because of texel:pixel ratio — the blade map is 256 texels/u so it is 1:1 at ~5 u and minified from every camera beyond, and its 127-cycle term (the only content fine enough to read from a 1.5-u camera) was pushed from 0.5 to 0.8 while the sum's amplitude dropped to hold the total. `TURF_BLADE_MIX` 0.85 → **0.5**, `bumpScale` 0.35 → **0.22** (a bump map is differentiated per screen pixel, so an over-driven map stipples the SHADING too), `anisotropy` 8 → 16 on both.
*Result, measured with `probe-grass-shot.mjs` on `samples/grass-check.tsx` (per-pixel Laplacian energy of a 1:1 520×400 crop, mean luminance ~76-82):* closeup —, eye 16.5 → 8.3, near 12.9 → 5.5, mid 13.9 → 4.6, overview 19.3 → 5.2, nadir 14.3 → 4.6. **A 2-4× drop in per-pixel energy at every distance**, with the broad read preserved by mixing `patchAt` harder (0.40/0.46 → 0.55/0.62) now that it has a mid-band octave to mix. Draw calls, triangles and fps unchanged; +1 canvas resolution step (~+200 ms one-time build, +4 MB texture).
*Known limit, not fixed.* From ~1.5 u (nose in the lawn) the ground is a soft wash: nothing in a tiled 512² detail map is fine enough at 870 screen px per world unit, and adding content that fine to the MACRO map is what caused this bug. Real blade-scale detail there needs geometry or parallax, not another noise octave.

Judge changes here by LOOKING: `node harness/park-eval/probe-grass-shot.mjs samples/grass-check.tsx --tag=x` (six fixed distances, each written as a full frame AND a 1:1 crop, with the Laplacian numbers above) or `node harness/park-eval/eval.mjs samples/grass-check.tsx` (a terrain-only probe park at the default size) and read `01-04` for the orbit read, `06-ground` for the grazing read and `06b-ground-close` for the eye-level read. `harness/park-eval/probe-ground-mat.mjs` reports the material and the texture histogram numerically when a screenshot is ambiguous.

**FIRM SHORE ON A MOUNTAINOUS PLOT.** `firmShore` floors every cell below `waterLevel + 0.34`, which on a real landform is close to half the plot. Past a 48-u plot the mirrored rise is AMPLITUDE-RELATIVE (55% of the dip, capped at 0.42× the landform amplitude) instead of the old flat 0.5-u cap — that cap was the single biggest cause of "the land is nearly flat", because it mapped half the plot into one 0.5-u band. ≤48 keeps the old constants exactly.

Pair with `buildWater` from components/WaterTile placed at the water level for lakes, and `buildRock` / `buildRockCluster` for outcrops. Built with three.js on the shared Stage (`ground={false}`).
