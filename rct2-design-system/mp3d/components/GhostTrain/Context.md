# GhostTrain

**CANONICAL IMPORT — copy exactly:** `import { GhostTrain } from './components/GhostTrain';`
*(never from `'./Park'`: only the `<Park>` wrappers + track-piece JSX live there. A wrong
specifier makes esbuild refuse the WHOLE bundle — the round-8 black-page failure.)*

Ghost train dark ride: a dark stepped-gable show building (skull-and-crossbones sign, flame lanterns, blood-red flap doors placed exactly where the spline pierces the wall) on a closed CatmullRom circuit that runs through the building and dips outdoors through a mini graveyard — carved tombstones, a leaning cross, a dead tree and a rusty iron fence arc. A 3-car train (2-seat cars with cushioned benches + shared lap bars, lamp and skull emblem on the lead car) rides the twin tube rails via a minimal local spline runner. Night is eerie green: interior door-glow, graveyard wash and the gated head-lamp (3 real PointLights) plus flickering green panes.

Built with three.js on the shared Stage; modelled on the RCT2 Ghost Train silhouette.

## Size and detail pass

The whole ride is built inside a **shell scaled by `RIDE_SCALE` (1.3)** — every dimension in the source is authored in the original units and the shell enlarges the assembly as one, so building, graveyard, track and train stay in exact proportion (the RCT2 2×2 dark ride reads as a 3×3). Two things deliberately do NOT scale with it: decorative riders are pre-divided by `RIDE_SCALE` so guests stay one size park-wide, and the entrance hut is re-placed by `<ConfigurableRide>` from the measured footprint.

What the detail pass added, in the order it reads from the path:

**The skull and the ghosts are a SECOND pass, and one observation drives both.** At the size these are actually seen — a 0.34-wide sign read across a park, a 0.1 emblem on a car's nose, a ghost at 40 pixels — the pale bone/cloth is a **blob** and **the dark shapes are the entire signal**. So the darks are sized first and the form is built around them, against the read rather than against realism. (The first pass sized the bone and then dotted features onto it; that is why it read as an egg and a marshmallow.)

- **The skull** (`buildSkull(k)`, shared by the facade sign and the lead car's nose emblem): the **orbits are sized first** — about a THIRD of the face wide each (0.085 × 0.105, against the first pass's 0.05-radius dots on a 0.17 cranium), canted inward and down the way orbits sit under a brow, and **sunk** so the bone surface cuts the opening rather than a dark ball sitting proud of it. The nose is a real **inverted-heart nasal aperture** — two angled slots meeting at a septum — not a small cone. The **tooth line is read from the dark GAPS**: white teeth on white bone are invisible, so it is a pale band with dark vertical slots cut into it over a dark mouth shadow (one slot missing, one tooth gone). The bone is then built round those darks: a cranium WIDER THAN TALL with a squared brow shelf, temple flats and an occiput (the taper is what separates a skull from an egg in silhouette), a nasal bridge, zygomatic cheek arches and a mandible. The bone is the brightest tone in the component so it pops off the dark board. Mounted over two real bones (`buildBone` — shaft plus four knuckle knobs) on a bevelled board with corner bolts.
- **Three ghosts** (`buildGhost(k, alpha)`): **silhouette-first** — a narrow head over SLOPING shoulders over a shroud that FLARES roughly 1:2 (head 0.09 against a 0.19 hem) down to a **hem of 8 deep scallops**, every third drawn out into a **drip**; a **trailing wisp** off the back so it reads as floating and not standing; and tapered **draped sleeves** thrown up and out (the old round "hands" were what made it a snowman). The face is **three big dark shapes** — two eyes each a third of the face wide and a full open howl of a mouth — which is the entire read at distance. Cloth is translucent and faintly self-lit, glowing harder on the night gate. One ghost is penned behind the bars of the bay window, one rises THROUGH the roof ridge and a wraith drifts a slow circle between the tombstones.
- **Roof**: five staggered courses of slate tabs per slope (55 boxes a slope, merged to one draw call each) on a deterministic weathered sag, over the deck.
- **Walls**: a stone plinth course, brick buttresses up both flanks with pale caps and cast-iron downpipes with hopper heads — without them the flanks were 2.3 × 1.5 slabs of flat colour. The wall tone is deliberately lifted off near-black so the shaded flank keeps its relief.
- **Cobwebs**: real quarter-webs (radial spokes plus rings that sag between them, merged) in three wall corners.
- **Graveyard**: four tombstones in three head shapes (round, gabled, snapped-off with the broken piece fallen beside it), each with a plinth, a recessed carved panel with chiselled inscription lines, a kerbed grave slab and moss; a leaning cross; a toppled urn; weed tufts; and a dead tree grown as a real branching skeleton — a kinked, buttressed bole and two generations of limbs, each child growing off its parent's TIP so the joints meet.

## RCT2 station behaviour (motion gate) + real seats

Capacity **6** = 3 two-seat cars (was 4). REAL GameManager guests board DISTINCT live seat anchors via `seatWorld` (decorative riders default true standalone / **false when registered** — unfilled seats read visibly EMPTY). The built `update` is wrapped in `createMotionGate` (GameManager): a registered ride sits **PARKED** through `waitingForPassengers`/`waitingToDepart`/`unloadingPassengers`, eases 0→1 into motion on `departing` (~0.8 s) and eases back to rest into `arriving` — so guests board and leave a stationary vehicle, RCT2 Vehicle.cpp-style. Breakdown spin-downs compose with the gate (the FSM parks in `movingToEndOfStation` while broken). Un-registered previews are byte-identical: the gate starts UNGATED and passes raw time through until the first `onStateChange`. The train's crawl clock is gated: it stands parked on the loop for boarding/unloading.
